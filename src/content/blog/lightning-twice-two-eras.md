## WWW

I am fortunate enough (read: old enough) to be a professional programmer during two world-changing technology shifts.

The first tech milestone I experienced was the genesis of the World Wide Web. I watched as the internet figured out what it was to be.

I was there, learning it as it came online, building HTML and JavaScript things with capabilities that had barely existed the month before.

Back then, we developers could feel the trajectory even before we built it. First came web pages with images, then movement with JavaScript and Flash, then video. Each step felt like the obvious next chapter in a story already written.

I never doubted where it was headed. I only wondered with impatience how fast we could go.

## AI

Now, we are wading in the Artificial Intelligence Era.

I'm curious and excited again in a way I haven't been since the advent of the WWW, showing up every day wanting to know what's new, what's possible now that wasn't possible last week.

This ground-breaking shift feels different, though. There is no single forecast of what's ahead.

Even the scariest moment of the World Wide Web era looks almost quaint in hindsight.

In 1999, the entire industry prepared and braced for Y2K.

Planes were supposedly going to fall out of the sky; money: unretrievable from banks; systems everywhere might fail.

And then midnight came. The lights stayed on. Planes stayed in the air.

The implications, speculation, and future of AI don't give us that same clarity or the comfort of a concrete resolution that Y2K did.

This era feels different; this time, it has a shape of deep uncertainty.

It's a strange and fascinating thing, getting to be part of two game-changing technological eras like this.

The first time, I participated in the world becoming connected; this time, I'm watching machines begin to understand.

It's thrilling in a way I didn't expect to feel again - and, maybe a bit scary.

Because this time, none of us know how the story will unfold.
